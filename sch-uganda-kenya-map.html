Schistosomiasis at the Uganda–Kenya Border

Every implementation unit in Uganda and Kenya, shaded by the schistosomiasis prevalence class each programme reported to ESPEN for 2024. The 20 IUs that meet across the international boundary are outlined in red. Read together they show something other than a prevalence gradient: for SCH, most of this border is a reporting gap.

The border is mostly a blind spot

Only six of the twenty border IUs carry a resolved prevalence band, and all six sit in one place — the Lake Victoria corner around Busia and Tororo. Everything from Mount Elgon north to the South Sudan tri-point is Unknown or an unresolvable “less than 10%”. Of the 17 places where a Ugandan district actually abuts a Kenyan sub-county, 11 have at least one side reporting nothing and four are blind on both sides at once — including the whole Karamoja–Turkana pastoralist corridor, where cross-border movement is highest and surveillance is thinnest.

What the map shows

The schistosomiasis prevalence class each national programme reported to ESPEN for 2024, at implementation-unit level: districts in Uganda (146 IUs), sub-counties in Kenya (290 IUs). Class sets MDA frequency — ≥50% warrants twice-yearly praziquantel, 10–49% annual, 1–9% every other year, and <1% moves to surveillance.

Rows are the TargetPop = Total slice. Endemicity is identical across the PreSAC, SAC, Adults and Total rows for every IU in both countries, so the choice does not affect the classes drawn here — only the population counts.

Two vocabularies, one scale

ESPEN carries an older and a newer classification side by side. “High prevalence (50% and above)” and “Prevalence 50% and above” are the same band, as are the two 10–49% wordings, and both are folded together here.

One does not fold. The old “Low prevalence (less than 10%)” spans both the newer 1–9% and <1% bands, and that is exactly the line between continuing treatment and stopping it. 64 IUs — 44 Kenyan, 20 Ugandan, five of them on the border — sit in it. They are drawn in neutral grey rather than guessed into a band.

How border IUs were identified

ESPEN publishes attribute data without geometry, so IUs were joined by name to open district and sub-county boundaries — 436 of 436 matched — then every Ugandan polygon was measured against every Kenyan one. Pairs within 500 m are treated as sharing the border; the tolerance absorbs misalignment between two independently drawn boundary sets.

This is the land border. Budalangi (Busia County, Kenya) fronts the boundary inside Lake Victoria and is not outlined.

Caveats

Uganda's nine city IUs have no separate polygon in the 2020 boundary set and are drawn with their parent district. Where a city and its parent disagree the polygon takes the higher class and the tooltip says so; seven disagree for SCH. None of them touch the border, so the border analysis is unaffected.

Coverage above 100% is reported as published — it means more people were treated than the denominator projected, not an error introduced here.
